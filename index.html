PROPOSED CITY ORDINANCE NO. 001
November 18, 2026 · Series of 2026
Proposed measure · For classroom deliberation only

An Ordinance Establishing Equal Opportunity, Fair Participation, and Accountability in Programs Funded, Administered, or Sponsored by the Local Government, Prohibiting Gender-Based Discrimination Therein, and Providing Mechanisms for Implementation and Redress

Be it ordained by the Sangguniang Panlungsod of the Fictional City of Maharlika, in session assembled, that:

WHEREAS, every person is entitled to equal dignity, fair treatment, and meaningful access to public opportunities and services, regardless of sex, gender identity, or gender expression;

WHEREAS, gender stereotypes and discriminatory practices may prevent qualified individuals from participating in livelihood, educational, sports, and community programs;

WHEREAS, the local government has a responsibility, within its lawful authority, to ensure that programs under its funding or administration are accessible, transparent, and fairly implemented;

NOW, THEREFORE, the Sangguniang Panlungsod hereby proposes the following ordinance:

Contents
Article I — General Provisions Article II — Equal Opportunity and Prohibited Acts Article III — Implementation and Accountability Article IV — Complaints and Remedies Article V — Penalties and Liability Article VI — Funding and Final Provisions

Article I

General Provisions

Section 1. Short Title. — This measure shall be known as the “PANTAY-AMBAG Ordinance.”

Section 2. Declaration of Policy. — The City shall promote equal opportunity and fair participation in local public programs. No qualified person shall be excluded, disadvantaged, or assigned a role solely because of sex, gender identity, gender expression, or a gender stereotype.

Section 3. Scope and Application. — This Ordinance shall apply to City offices and to programs funded, administered, or officially sponsored by the City, including:

(a) Livelihood, employment-readiness, and skills-training programs;(b) Sports, recreation, competitions, and community activities;(c) Youth, educational, and civic-participation programs; and(d) The distribution of local assistance, benefits, and public services.

A private contractor or partner organization shall be covered only in relation to its performance of a City-funded or City-administered program and subject to its agreement with the City. This Ordinance shall not regulate private conduct unrelated to such programs or supersede the authority of national agencies.

Section 4. Definition of Terms. — For purposes of this Ordinance:

(a) Gender-based discrimination refers to an unjust exclusion, restriction, or unequal treatment of a person because of sex, gender identity, or gender expression.(b) Gender stereotyping refers to assigning abilities, duties, or roles to a person based solely on generalized beliefs about a particular gender.(c) Program implementer refers to a City office, official, employee, contractor, or partner organization responsible for carrying out or managing a covered program.

Article II

Equal Opportunity and Prohibited Acts

Section 5. Right to Equal Opportunity. — Every qualified person shall have a fair opportunity to apply for and participate in a covered program. Selection shall be based on relevant, reasonable, and publicly stated criteria, applied consistently to all applicants.

Nothing in this Section shall prohibit lawful and reasonable measures intended to address documented barriers or inequalities affecting particular groups, provided that such measures are consistent with national law.

Section 6. Prohibited Acts. — No program implementer shall:

(a) Deny or restrict a qualified applicant's participation solely because of sex, gender identity, or gender expression;(b) Assign duties or responsibilities solely on the belief that an activity is appropriate only for a particular gender;(c) Impose an additional fee, condition, or qualification on an applicant because of gender;(d) Use a gender stereotype as the sole basis for selection, assignment, suspension, or removal from a covered program; or(e) Threaten, intimidate, disadvantage, or retaliate against a person for making a good-faith report or participating in an inquiry under this Ordinance.

Section 7. Lawful and Objective Requirements. — A requirement directly imposed by law, or reasonably necessary to meet a documented safety or program objective, shall not by itself constitute discrimination, provided that it is proportionate, supported by objective reasons, and applied fairly. A gender stereotype alone shall never be sufficient justification.

Article III

Implementation and Accountability

Section 8. Pre-Implementation Review. — Before launching a covered program, the responsible City office shall:

(a) Publish the eligibility and selection criteria in an accessible manner;(b) Review the application process and program rules for unjustified gender-based restrictions; and(c) Identify a contact point and procedure for raising concerns or complaints.

The responsible office shall complete a Gender-Fairness Checklist for each covered program. The checklist shall be reviewed by the City's Gender and Development Focal Point System or its duly designated representative.

Section 9. Monitoring and Annual Report. — The GAD Focal Point System shall coordinate monitoring and prepare an annual report describing implementation, identified barriers, complaints received, corrective actions, and recommendations. Public reports shall use aggregated information and shall not identify complainants or disclose sensitive personal information.

Section 10. Protection of Personal Information. — Personal information collected under this Ordinance shall be limited to what is necessary, processed only for a lawful purpose, protected against unauthorized access or disclosure, and handled in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173, or the Data Privacy Act of 2012, and its implementing rules. Disclosure of gender identity or other sensitive personal information shall not be required unless there is a lawful and necessary basis.

Article IV

Complaints, Investigation, and Remedies

Section 11. Filing of Complaints. — Any person who experiences or witnesses a possible violation may submit a complaint to the GAD Focal Point System or to an office designated by the City Mayor. A complaint may be made in writing or orally. An authorized representative may submit a complaint on behalf of an affected person, subject to appropriate safeguards.

Section 12. Complaint Procedure. — Upon receipt of a complaint, the receiving office shall:

(a) Acknowledge receipt within three (3) working days;(b) Conduct an initial assessment within ten (10) working days;(c) Give the person complained of written notice of the allegations and a reasonable opportunity to respond; and(d) Complete fact-finding within thirty (30) working days, unless an extension is justified in writing and notice is given to the parties.

The process shall be impartial, confidential to the extent allowed by law, and consistent with due process. No complainant shall be forced to confront or settle with the person complained of as a condition for receiving or pursuing a complaint. Matters that may involve violations of national law shall be referred promptly to the appropriate authority.

Section 13. Interim Protection and Non-Retaliation. — Where reasonably necessary to prevent further harm or interference with an inquiry, the responsible office may recommend temporary, proportionate measures that do not presume the guilt of any person. Retaliation against a complainant, witness, or person assisting an inquiry is prohibited and shall be treated as a separate violation.

Section 14. Corrective Measures. — Where a violation is established after the appropriate process, the City may require:

(a) Correction or removal of the discriminatory rule, criterion, or decision;(b) Reopening of an application or restoration of a fair opportunity to participate, where practicable;(c) Relevant gender-sensitivity training for responsible program personnel; and(d) Referral of a City official or employee for the applicable administrative process.

For a contractor or partner organization, corrective action, suspension, or termination of an agreement may be considered only in accordance with the contract, applicable procurement rules, national law, and due process.

Article V

Penalties and Liability

Section 15. Liability and Penalties. — Any individual who, without lawful justification, knowingly commits an act prohibited under Section 6 or knowingly retaliates against a complainant or witness shall, upon conviction by a court of competent jurisdiction, be punished by a fine not exceeding Five Thousand Pesos (₱5,000.00), or imprisonment for a period not exceeding one (1) year, or both, at the discretion of the court, consistent with the penalty limit for city ordinances under Section 458(a)(1)(iii) of Republic Act No. 7160. No person shall be penalized under this Section without proof of the prohibited act and observance of due process.

City officials and employees shall be subject to the administrative rules and disciplinary procedures applicable to their positions. Contractors and partner organizations may be subject to contractual remedies under Section 14. Conduct that may constitute an offense under national law shall be referred to the competent authority; this Ordinance shall not create or replace a national criminal offense.

Article VI

Funding and Final Provisions

Section 16. Appropriations. — The funds necessary for implementation shall be charged against the City's approved Gender and Development Plan and Budget and other lawful appropriations, subject to existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules.

Section 17. Implementing Rules. — Within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Ordinance, the City Mayor, through the GAD Focal Point System and concerned offices, shall issue the necessary implementing guidelines. Such guidelines shall not diminish the rights or protections established by this Ordinance or alter a penalty fixed by the Sangguniang Panlungsod.

Section 18. Separability Clause. — If any provision of this Ordinance is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue in full force and effect.

Section 19. Repealing Clause. — All local ordinances, rules, or regulations inconsistent with this Ordinance are hereby amended or repealed only to the extent of such inconsistency, subject to applicable law.

Section 20. Effectivity. — This Ordinance shall take effect ten (10) days after a copy is posted in the places required by Section 59 of Republic Act No. 7160, or the Local Government Code of 1991, and after compliance with any publication requirement applicable to an ordinance carrying penal sanctions.
